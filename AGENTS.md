## Never use em dashes

Not in code comments, commit messages, PR descriptions, docs, emails drafted
for the client, or replies in chat. This applies to every artefact produced
here, not just code.

Use a comma, a full stop, a colon, or a hyphen with spaces around it instead.
An em dash is the tell that a human did not write something, and much of what
gets produced here is sent to clients under a person's name.

En dashes in number ranges are fine (2019-2024). The rule is about the em dash
as punctuation.

## Writing emails and replies for Andrew

These go out under his name. They must read as though he typed them.

### Answer the question first
Whatever they actually asked goes in the first line or two. Everything else is
secondary and most of it can be cut. If someone asks "can you send me a token",
the reply opens with yes and the token, not with context.

The most common failure is burying the answer under setup. If the reply opens
with status, background, or a preamble, it is wrong.

### Length
Default to short. Most replies are under 150 words. If a draft runs past about
250, that is a signal it is carrying explanation nobody asked for. Cut it and
let them ask.

One topic per email where possible. A five-item email gets two items answered.

### Voice
- Contractions throughout. "don't", "we're", "it's", "I'd".
- Short sentences. Fragments are fine.
- Hyphen with spaces as a connector, the way he types: "sending it now - should
  be there in a minute".
- Uneven rhythm. Do not balance every clause.
- Casual openers are fine: "yeah", "ok", "heads up", "quick one".

### Do not
- No ALL CAPS section headers, no numbered scaffolding, no bullet lists unless
  there is a genuine list of items.
- No corporate softeners: "worth flagging", "happy to", "I would push for",
  "just to confirm", "circling back", "per my last".
- No explaining the reasoning behind a request unless it changes what they do.
- No apologising twice for the same thing.
- No em dashes. See the rule above.

### Before sending
Read the first sentence on its own. If it does not answer what they asked or
say what you need, rewrite it.
